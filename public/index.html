      9. How to teach and use thisDaily steps, the six stages, every feature
    

      Every day, at every stage
      
    
        
  1. Talk and tap. Say your sentence out loud and tap one or two key words on the board as you say them. "Time to EAT."
    2. 
        
  2. Use Show me. Hold the Show me button at the top for one second first. Your taps then speak the word and show it, but do not build a sentence and are not counted as your child's words. It turns itself off after 90 quiet seconds.
    3. 
        
  3. Stay one step ahead. If your child uses one word, show two: WANT + EAT.
    4. 
        
  4. Ask, then wait. Count to five in your head before you help. Look at your child, then at the board.
    5. 
        
  5. Answer every tap. Even an accident gets a real response. A tap on UP: you say "You want up!" and lift them.
    6. 
        
  6. Add a little. Your child says EAT. You say and tap EAT + MORE.
    7. 
        
  7. Do not quiz. Skip "show me eat." Use it in real moments: meals, play, bath, the car.
    8. 
        
  8. Keep it in reach, always. Signs, pointing and sounds still count too.
    9. 
      


      The six stages
      
    
        
  1. First words. 10 pictures. One tap says the word, then a full sentence. Start here if the board is new.
    2. 
        
  2. More words. 20 pictures in the same places. Two taps make a bigger sentence: NO + PLAY.
    3. 
        
  3. Word pages. Tabs for People, Food, Feelings, Places and Things, plus a Questions page of whole questions to ask with one tap. You choose the questions under "Questions page" above.
    4. 
        
  4. Building sentences. Adds I, you, it and not in a new left column. "not" makes it a no. "it" stands for the thing. "you" says just what was tapped: YOU + PLAY says "You play."
    5. 
        
  5. My own sentences. A typing line. Whatever was just said lands there, ready to fix and say again. The star saves it to the My own page. The X on a saved sentence deletes it (press twice).
    6. 
        
  6. Teen and adult words. Four pages of the words older kids and adults say most. No pictures. Here the talker says the words in the order tapped and only tidies small things, like adding "am" or a question mark.
    7. 
      

      The 20 home words stay in the same places at every stage, so nothing already learned moves. Move up when your child is using most of what is there, not by age. Moving up early does no harm: extra words can be ignored until they are needed. Ask your speech therapist if you are unsure. This advice on when to move up is common practice, not a tested rule.

      What the talker does by itself
      
    
        
  1. It listens. No button. When someone asks your child a yes or no question, a this-or-that question, or "what do you want to drink?", big answers pop up. Set this under "When a question is heard".
    2. 
        
  2. It knows color questions. "What color do you want?" brings up eight colors at every stage: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, brown and black. If the person names the colors ("red or green?"), it offers those instead.
    3. 
        
  3. It knows number questions. "How many do you want?", "How old are you?" and "Pick a number" bring up 1 to 10 at every stage. So do school math questions like "What is 2 plus 2?". From Stage 3 up there is a Numbers page (0 to 9, plus o'clock, dollars, years old, minutes, times) and a "Bigger number" button that opens it. There, digits tapped in a row make any number: 1 then 2 says "I am 12."
    4. 
        
  4. It can ask an AI for help. Off by default. Turn on "Use AI for harder questions" and questions the rules miss, like "how was school?", get answer buttons matched to the stage. Only the words heard are sent.
    5. 
        
  5. It glows. As people talk, the pictures for the words they say light up on the board. That shows where words live without anyone pointing.
    6. 
        
  6. It shows the written word for 3 seconds on every tap, so the spelling is seen with the sound.
    7. 
        
  7. It keeps count. "Progress" above shows which words are used. Press Copy report and send it to the therapist.
    8. 
      


      Things you set up
      
    
        
  1. Photo scenes. Add a real photo from your child's life and put word spots on it. "Suggest words" finds things in the photo for you.
    2. 
        
  2. Personal words. "Add a word" for names, favorite foods and places. "Change a word" swaps any picture or name.
    3. 
        
  3. Choices offered. Pick up to six words per group for open questions like "what do you want to eat?"
    4. 
        
  4. Teen and adult wording. One switch changes potty to bathroom, teddy to phone and so on. It turns on by itself at Stages 5 and 6.
    5. 
        
  5. Seeing and hearing. High contrast, bigger pictures or words, more space between pictures, a clear sign when the talker is speaking, and vibration on tap. Ask the vision or hearing specialist which fit.
    6. 
        
  6. Touch settings. If a hand rests on the screen or taps come in twos, adjust "Ignore extra taps", "How long a picture must be held" and "Ignore touches at the edges".
    7. 
        
  7. Child lock. Keeps your child in the talker. For the home button use Guided Access on an iPad or Pin app on a Galaxy.
    8. 
        
  8. Typing. Can be turned on at any stage for a child who reads early.
    9.
